# 2026-08-27 — The Stolen Psalter

**At the beginning** it looked like an ordinary theft.

**According to the legend**, the monastery stood on **sacred land** where a **warrior** once knelt after a battle. For nine hundred years the **brotherhood** had guarded one **unique** book — a hand-painted Psalter, the **heritage** of the whole valley. Last Tuesday it vanished **under mysterious circumstances**.

Detective Nadia's plane was delayed, so she was **late for** the first briefing, and she was afraid of every **takeoff** anyway — she had read about one **plane crash** too many. **On the way** from the airport she thought of nothing else, **until** she **arrived at** the monastery gate.

Her motel room had a **cockroach** in the sink, and she had forgotten her **toothpaste**. *That's really reassuring*, she thought.

**The next day** she began to **examine** the scene. The abbot showed her the empty case; **those** old locks, he said, had never been broken before. She collected what **evidence** she could — a torn **cord**, a smear of wax, and one real **clue**: a single **frame** from a **security camera**, showing a figure in the corridor at three in the morning.

The **original mirror** at the end of that corridor had caught what the camera missed. The **reflection** had a **completely different** face.

Her first **suspect** was a student from the visiting **sorority**, but she **turned out to be** asleep in her room all night. Slowly they **came to the conclusion** that someone inside had helped.

The abbot spoke of the thief with quiet **wrath**. "He was one of us. We **sent** him **out of** the brotherhood for **immorality**, and he swore to **take revenge on** us."

"For money?" Nadia asked.

"No. It was **a mindset**. He was a **scoffer** — he held everything holy **in derision**. Men like that never **prosper**. They are **chaff**, and the **wind** drives them away."

They found his **lair** in an abandoned hunting cabin, where a stuffed **deer** watched from the wall. He had been using the book to **lure** collectors. *All it can do is steal*, he had written in his notebook, and he meant himself.

Nadia decided to **take on** the arrest herself. She had to **get out of** the car quietly and **carry out** the plan before dawn.

But the man had **plotted** for months. When he saw her, his anger was instantly **kindled**; he began to **rage**, and tried to **dash** the Psalter against the stones — **in vain**. She was faster. He was **wounded** in the struggle, though not badly, and afterwards **almost all of them** — monks, police, the whole valley — wanted him **executed** under the old law.

He was not. **In spite of** everything, the brotherhood offered him **refuge**.

Nadia watched him **transform into** something smaller and quieter as **the sun came up**. The **wicked**, she thought, are rarely monsters. They mostly just get tired.

The valley **regained** its book. **Engulfed in** morning light, the hill looked almost innocent — no **fury**, no **flames**, no judgment. Only stone, and an old song about kings who plot in vain.
