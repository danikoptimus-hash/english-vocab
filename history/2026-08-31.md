## 1. The Brother They Sold

Genesis, the first book of the **Pentateuch**, tells a story that stays modern.

Jacob had twelve sons and loved the eleventh best. The **envy** of the older brothers grew quietly for years.

They **lured** Joseph away from the camp with a story about sheep, made him **shed** his coloured robe, and threw him into a dry **pit**. The **betrayal** was **intentionally** planned. **One of the group members**, Reuben, argued against it; nobody **took** him **seriously**. The eldest spoke **in their names**, and their father believed him.

In Egypt he rose fast, then fell. He **was accused of** forcing himself on his master's wife — of an **affair** he had refused — and years in prison for it. The charge was **nonsense**, but nobody investigated. He was the perfect **victim**: foreign, young, alone.

Then came the **famine**. Joseph, now the second man in Egypt, controlled the grain.

His brothers arrived **in search of** food and did not know him: **disguised** by twenty years and by power, he was the exact **opposite** of the boy in the pit. The meetings were **tense**; twice he left the room to hide his face.

The **attempt** to stay cold failed. When Judah offered himself in place of the youngest brother, Joseph was **overwhelmed** and wept so loudly the servants heard.

He was not **vengeful**. He forgave them **graciously**: *you meant it for evil, but it became good.*

It is **apparent** that the story is less about forgiveness than about a **former** victim choosing what kind of man to be. Their **fraternity** had **barely** survived; for a while it **seemed** dead. Their **descendants** became a nation anyway.

That night they said a **prayer** of thanks, and I imagine the old father **chanting** it in the dark, not believing his son was alive.

## 2. The Cord

**After another case** that went nowhere, Alvarez had stopped believing in coincidence.

The first body lay in **a secluded place** off the road. The man had **pulled over** to change a tyre. His car had **caught fire** and was still **engulfed in** flames when patrol arrived. At the **accident scene** they found a length of blue nylon **cord** in the grass — too clean to have lain there.

The second victim was **an air traffic controller** driving home from a night shift. Same road, same **cord**, same length. That car went off the bridge, and **he ended up under water**.

That was the **pattern**. Someone had been **stalking** drivers on that road for two years: cut a **wire** so the engine dies, wait for the car to stop, use the **cord**, and let the fire cover the rest.

Alvarez filed the **form** for a search and drove out at dusk with **a shotgun** on the seat.

Something **stung** her neck in the ditch. She did not **scare** easily, but the silence did the work: trees **surrounded** the road, and there was no signal.

She knew what she **was dealing with**. Not a monster: a man with a knife, a spool of **cord**, and two years of practice.
