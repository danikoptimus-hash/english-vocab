## The Deputy's Furnace

For a **decade** after the **famine** of the thirties, Cold Creek had nothing at all. Then the rain came back, the **flock** on the ridge doubled, and the town began calling those years its **prosperity**. Almost everyone here is the **offspring** of somebody who chose to stay through the bad decade.

Martha Voss owned the best **property** above the creek — eighty hectares, a barn, and an old coal **furnace** in its cellar that had not been lit since her father's time.

The trouble began when **Deputy** Ellis started to **harass** her. He came about fences, then about dogs, then about nothing anybody could name. He was an **intimidating** man with a **flawless** record: twenty-two years in the county, not one complaint on file.

Martha had **fallen in love with** a schoolteacher named Arlo. They announced their **engagement** in June, and she meant to **marry** him before the first snow and leave the ridge to their children.

Ellis was **furious**. He had wanted that land for years — the county intended to **demolish** the old barn and build on the site.

In August somebody **ran over** Arlo's dog on the county road and did not stop. Two weeks later three men **kidnapped** Arlo from the school car park, and before morning he had been **beaten to death** with **a baseball bat**.

Ellis **chose** to **cover up** the crime. They **dumped** the body in the reservoir and **burned** the schoolhouse **down** to explain the missing register. Whatever would not burn there went into the **furnace** under Martha's own barn — and that was the mistake that finished him.

She found the box thirteen months later: a **jumble** of half-melted buckles, a wristwatch, and one school key.

She walked **toward** the sheriff's office at four in the morning with the box under her coat. **The lights** were still burning inside; one of them **flickered**, and a thin **ray** came through the blind and lay across her hands while she waited at the desk.

Ellis tried to **drive** her **away** — first with paperwork, then with the same **cruel** patience he had spent on her fences for two years. She did not move from the chair.

The trial turned on a single question: the **sufficiency** of what was in that box. Three objects, no witnesses, and a **flawless** officer on the stand. It was very nearly not enough.

It was enough. A town that had been afraid of one man for a **decade** **defeated** him in eleven days, and something in Cold Creek **awakened** that has not gone back to sleep since.
